Leaderboard Office Methodology

Methodology. How the households, the office, the agents and the score fit together. This page is a placeholder and will change.

It's March 2026. A household hands over its 2025 paperwork, and an agent gets an office to work in: IRS publications, case law, employer portals, a basement of old returns, and a phone that rings the client. It reads, searches, asks, runs numbers, and writes a strategy. We score the strategy in dollars against the best one we know exists, then look at what it never opened.

A rollout

A rollout is one agent working one household until it says it's done. That's dozens of tool calls (open a source, search a shelf, call the client, run the engine) and a written strategy at the end. We run one or two per agent per household. The second pass is optional: the strategy comes back with flags and the agent is told to grill its own work and resubmit. Many more rollouts than that is a post-training budget, not an eval budget.

Capability and disposition

They come apart. Osprey recognizes an opportunity more reliably than anything else on the board once the facts are in front of it, and it finishes near the bottom. It rarely leaves the folder and almost never calls the client. The money in these households sits one or two hops from the papers the client hands over, and some of it is only in the client's head.

What counts as the agent's fault

Every source in the office was available. If the agent never opened the box with the 2022 return in it, the amendment it didn't write is on the agent, not the client.

Multi-agent

The world exposes plain tools, and it doesn't care who calls them. A planner can fan out readers against the same office. The harness supports it; we don't test it here.

The numbers

Each opportunity is the change in net federal tax from one engine, measured alone against a reference return, with FICA added where a payroll route applies. Values interact a little, so the best known strategy is a reference point rather than a sum you could always collect. The households and every value in them are generated by scripts/py/build.py; the JavaScript engine that grades in your browser is checked against it in CI.

Everything here is simulated: the households, the market, and the agents. The tax law is real, 2025 rules including the July 2025 bill.
